How a buffer works

A defined trade, set in advance.

A buffered fund uses options on the S&P 500 to give up some of the upside in exchange for a cushion on the downside.

01

A cap on the way up

The fund follows the S&P 500 up to a set limit for the period. In a very strong market, gains above the cap are the price of the cushion.

02

A buffer on the way down

If the index falls over the period, the fund is built to absorb the first part of the decline: the first 20% each quarter in BALT, and the first 10% over a year in each of BUFR's twelve funds.

03

A reset on a schedule

When a period ends, a new cap and buffer are set at current market levels. Between resets the funds can still move down with the market, usually by less.

A fair question

Why not just own bonds?

Bonds are the traditional way to steady a portfolio, and they still have a place. Buffered Growth takes a different route to the same goal, in three specific ways.

Bonds can fall with stocks.

In 2022, rising interest rates pushed stocks and bonds down together, and a bond fund offered little cushion. Buffered Growth does not depend on interest rates falling to hold its ground.

The trade is set in advance.

Each buffered fund sets its cap and its buffer at the start of a period. You can see what you are giving up and what you are getting before the period begins, though neither is guaranteed.

Growth still comes from stocks.

A bond's return is mostly the interest it pays. The buffered funds follow the S&P 500 up to their caps, so the portfolio keeps a link to the long-term growth of the stock market.

What the five years showed
The honest trade-offsThis portfolio costs more to own than an index fund (about 0.59% a year in fund expenses against about 0.03%). In strong years the caps hold it well behind the stock market. Buffers work over a set period, not day by day, and they are not guaranteed. A bond pays contractual interest and returns its principal at maturity, and this portfolio does neither. Its history is also short, a little over five years. We think the design is worth those costs for the right part of a plan. That is a judgment, and we are happy to walk through it with you.
Who it is for

Is Buffered Growth right for you?

A strong fit if you

  • Want your savings to keep growing, with smaller swings along the way
  • Would trade some upside in strong markets for a cushion in weak ones
  • Have relied on bonds for stability and want another approach for part of that money
  • Can leave this money invested for five years or more

Think twice if you

  • Want all of the stock market's upside and have decades to ride out the declines
  • Need a guarantee of principal or a guaranteed income
  • Need regular income from this account today
  • Expect a portfolio that never declines

Buffered funds. BALT and BUFR use options to seek a buffer against a set amount of decline in the S&P 500 over a set outcome period, in exchange for a cap on gains. Managed futures. DBMF uses futures contracts to follow market trends. It can decline when trends reverse, including in years when stocks rise. It fell 8.9% in 2023.
