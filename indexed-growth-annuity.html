Growth with a floor,

An indexed growth annuity earns interest tied to the S&P 500, up to a cap.
In a down year for the index, the credit is 0% and your contract value holds.

How it works

Three terms do the work.

An indexed growth annuity is a fixed indexed annuity built for accumulation. It is an insurance contract, not an investment in the stock market. Each year it follows a simple set of rules.

01  The cap

Your upside, up to a limit

Each contract year, your credit is the price return of the S&P 500, up to a cap. With a 10% cap, a year when the index rises 16% credits 10%. Once credited, that interest is locked in.

02  The floor

A down year credits 0%

When the index is down for the year, your credit is 0%. Your contract value does not decline because of the market, and the next year starts from where you already are.

03  The cost

No statement fee, no product fee

Nothing is deducted from your contract value each year. The cost is built into the contract instead, through the cap on your upside and limits on how much you can withdraw during the surrender period.

Where it fits

The steady part of a retirement portfolio.

The conservative part of a retirement portfolio has one job. It holds steady when stocks fall, so you have somewhere to draw income from while your stocks recover.

For many people within ten years of retirement, we use an indexed growth annuity for the work long-term bond funds used to do.
The same defensive job, done with a floor.

We are hybrid advisors, which means we can use investments and insurance side by side. It fits some plans and not others. Your advisor will show you where it belongs in yours, if it belongs at all.

  • Your upside is cappedIn a strong year, the index can earn far more than your credit.
    This is not a replacement for stocks.
  • Dividends are left outCredits follow the price return of the index, which does not include dividends.
  • Access is limited for a periodThese are long-term contracts. Most allow a free withdrawal each year, and withdrawals above that amount during the surrender period may carry surrender charges.
  • The cap can changeThe insurance company sets the cap at the start of each crediting period, and it can move up or down at renewal.
