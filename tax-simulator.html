What this simulator models

Federal, included. 2026 brackets and the standard deduction (Rev. Proc. 2025-32), the additional deduction at age 65, the $6,000 enhanced senior deduction under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act with its 6% phaseout, Social Security taxation through provisional income, capital gains and qualified dividends stacked at 0%, 15%, and 20%, simplified capital loss netting with the $3,000 limit, the 3.8% net investment income tax, the 0.9% additional Medicare tax on wages, and IRMAA tier placement on current-year MAGI.

Federal, not included. The alternative minimum tax, the qualified business income deduction, tax credits, dependents, the SALT cap inside itemized amounts, the tips and overtime deductions, and self-employment tax.

State. 2026 rates, brackets, standard deductions, and exemptions per the Tax Foundation (February 2026). Social Security is excluded automatically where a state exempts it, and the eight states that still tax benefits use simplified threshold rules. Retirement income is excluded automatically in Illinois, Pennsylvania, Mississippi, and Iowa (age 55 and older). The Missouri capital gains exclusion, the Washington capital gains excise, Montana's reduced long-term gain rates, and the Maryland capital gains surtax are included. Other state retirement exclusions, credits, itemized deductions, phaseouts, and recapture provisions are not, so use the state subtractions field and the note for your state. Local taxes apply only through the local rate you enter. Head of household uses single-filer state figures.

Important disclosures

Assumptions and limitations. This simulator is a hypothetical illustration for educational purposes only. It produces simplified, rounded planning estimates for tax year 2026 from the figures you enter. It is not a tax return, a tax projection prepared for you, or a recommendation, and your actual tax will differ. Federal figures follow IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act as described above. State figures follow the Tax Foundation's February 2026 tables, and several state rules are simplified or left out, as noted for each state. Tax law can change during the year.

Medicare. IRMAA tiers use the 2026 thresholds and premiums published by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services. Medicare premiums in any year are based on MAGI from two years prior, so 2026 income is shown against 2028 premiums using 2026 thresholds, which are adjusted each year. Part D amounts are the income-related surcharge added to your plan's premium.

Tax planning. Jantz Wealth Advisors does not provide tax or legal advice. Roth conversions are taxable events and cannot be undone, and realizing a capital gain is also a taxable event. Please consult a qualified tax professional before acting.
