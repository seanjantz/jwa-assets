The approach

Two jobs. Two tools.

Growing your money and protecting your retirement income are different jobs.
They call for different tools, and the tools are paid for differently.

Investments are the right tool for growth, flexibility, and liquidity. Insurance and annuities are the right tool for the problems investments can't solve as well: a paycheck that lasts as long as you do, and principal that holds steady in the years when a drawdown would do the most damage.

For most retirees and pre-retirees, a complete plan includes both. That is why we call ourselves a hybrid firm. It isn't a sales pitch. It is a description of the work. The two ways we're paid, an ongoing advisory fee and a one-time insurance commission, are simply the two kinds of work, paid for in the way that fits each.

The second way we're paid

Insurance commissions.
Paid by the carrier, once.

When an insurance product is placed as part of your plan, your advisor is compensated by the issuing insurance company, not by you. The commission comes from the carrier's own account and doesn't reduce your premium or your contract value. Rates vary by product and carrier, and you'll see the specifics before you sign anything.

The products we work with fall into three categories. Each one solves a different planning problem, and each has a defined role in your plan before it is ever recommended.

Category 01

Indexed growth annuities

The conservative part of your portfolio, with a floor long-term bond funds can't offer.

An indexed growth annuity is a fixed indexed annuity linked to a market index such as the S&P 500. It has a cap that limits how much you earn in any one year, a floor that keeps your principal from going backward, and no annual product fees. When the market is up, you participate up to the cap and the gain locks in. When the market is down, your contract value holds.

Every retirement portfolio needs a part that stays steady when stocks fall. For decades that job went to long-term bond funds. But there are years when long-term bond funds and stocks fall together, and 2022 is a recent example. A growth annuity doesn't have that failure mode. Its value is not exposed to market declines at all.

That is why, for most people within ten years of retirement, we use an indexed growth annuity for the work long-term bond funds used to do. It becomes the volatility buffer inside your portfolio, the sleeve you draw from in a down year so your stocks have time to recover. Same defensive job. Done with a floor. And because it carries no advisory fee, it changes the math on what you pay us, which we show below.

Category 02

Income annuities

A paycheck that lasts as long as you do.

An income annuity is built to produce guaranteed lifetime income, either right away or starting at a date you choose, for as long as you (or you and your spouse) are alive. The insurance company takes on the risk of you living a long time. You get a paycheck that doesn't depend on what the market does.

Social Security is one source of guaranteed lifetime income. A pension, if you have one, is another. For most people, those two don't fully cover the essential expenses they need covered no matter what. An income annuity fills that gap, and it lets the rest of your portfolio stay invested for growth because you aren't depending on it for groceries in a bad year.

The right amount depends on the size of your gap, not on a rule of thumb. We model your plan with it and without it before recommending a number.

Category 03

Life insurance

Tax-advantaged income, family protection, and living benefits.

Indexed universal life (IUL) is one of the more powerful tools in retirement planning and one of the most misunderstood. It builds cash value linked to a market index, with a floor against market declines, a death benefit that passes to your heirs income-tax-free, and the ability to access the cash value during your lifetime through policy loans that don't show up on your tax return. That matters for Medicare IRMAA brackets and for how much of your Social Security is taxed. For people who want tax-advantaged growth beyond what a 401(k) or IRA allows, and who have the time horizon to let it work, IUL is often the right answer. Policy design matters enormously, and you'll see a full illustration with realistic assumptions before any decision.

Term life is the simplest and least expensive form of life insurance, and the right answer for most families during their working years when there are dependents or debts to protect.

Living benefits. The life policies we recommend include riders, at no added cost, that let you access part of the death benefit while you are alive if you are diagnosed with a critical, chronic, or terminal illness. For many retirees, the chronic illness benefit can help with the costs of long-term care.

A lower total cost is a real consequence of the hybrid structure, and one an investment-only firm can't match because it won't use the tool. But the savings aren't the reason we recommend an indexed growth annuity. They are the result of recommending it when it is the right fit. The conservative part of a retirement portfolio has one job: hold steady when stocks fall. A growth annuity does that job with a floor under your principal. Long-term bond funds can't promise one. It gives up some liquidity and some upside to do it, which is why it fits some plans and not others. The lower fee is a welcome side effect.

How it fits together

Every dollar has a role.
Every tool has a job.

Fee-only advisors won't touch insurance and will send you to an agent if you need it. Insurance agents don't manage investments and will send you to an advisor for the portfolio. Either way, the two halves of your plan end up with people who don't talk to each other.

The hybrid approach is different. Your insurance and your investments are designed together, by one advisor, because the choices on one side change the choices on the other. A protected income floor lets your portfolio be positioned for growth. A growth annuity inside the portfolio lets the stocks ride out a bad year. Each piece does what it does best, and the plan is sturdier than either side could be on its own.

This is what we mean by hybrid advisor. It is why most of our clients have both.
