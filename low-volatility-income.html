How the paycheck works

A set amount, every month, reviewed once a year.

01

Collect

Each fund pays on its own schedule, most of them monthly. Everything they pay lands in the cash buffer instead of being spent as it arrives.

02

Pay

A set dollar amount transfers from the buffer to your bank account on the same day each month. It arrives whether the market had a good month or a bad one. A paycheck you can plan around.

03

Review

Once a year we look at what the funds paid and reset the check. In a good year it can go up. In a rough year it holds, and nothing has to be sold at the bottom to keep the bills paid.

Who it is for

Is Low-Volatility Income right for you?

A strong fit if you

  • Want a monthly check from your savings, starting now
  • Value a steady ride more than keeping up with the stock market
  • Can hold it in an IRA or other tax-advantaged account, where the income is not taxed each year
  • Already have growth money working somewhere else

Think twice if you

  • Do not need the income yet and want your money to grow instead
  • Need this account to keep pace with inflation over 20 or 30 years
  • Would be investing mostly in a taxable account
  • Expect it to match the stock market in strong years

Important disclosures

Hypothetical performance. The Low-Volatility Income portfolio was established in November 2026. All performance shown is hypothetical and does not represent any actual account. It was calculated by applying the portfolio's target weights (JEPI 25%, VCSH 20%, SCHD 15%, JAAA 15%, JEPQ 10%, SGOV 10% and JBBB 5%) to the historical monthly total returns and distributions of the underlying ETFs from January 1, 2022 through , rebalanced to target weights each January. Returns reflect each fund's internal expenses but not trading costs or taxes. JEPQ began trading in May 2022; from January through May 2022 the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ), which holds the same Nasdaq-100 stocks without the option income strategy, is used in its place. JBBB began trading on January 11, 2022, and its January 2022 return is measured from its first trading day. Hypothetical results do not reflect actual trading, were prepared with the benefit of hindsight, and may not reflect the impact that material economic and market factors might have had on decision-making if client money had actually been managed. No client has experienced these results. Price and distribution data: Yahoo Finance, retrieved November 2026.

Income estimates. The calculator's monthly and annual income estimates apply the weighted trailing 12-month distribution yield of the funds, as of the date shown, to the amount entered. They are not a projection or a guarantee. Fund distributions change with market conditions and interest rates. Distributions from JEPI and JEPQ come largely from option premiums and tend to fall when market volatility falls. Distributions from VCSH, JAAA, JBBB and SGOV move with interest rates. "Income paid out" figures assume every distribution is paid out in the month received; "income reinvested" figures assume every distribution is reinvested. An account that takes its income out should expect little or no growth in principal, and the principal can decline. Distributions from this portfolio are generally taxed as ordinary income in a taxable account.

Advisory fees. The maximum annual investment advisory fee is 1.25%. Results labeled "net of 1.25%" are shown after deducting a 1.25% annual advisory fee, applied quarterly in arrears as 0.3125% of the portfolio value at the end of each calendar quarter. Calculator figures are shown net of the fee you enter, applied the same way. Results labeled "before fees" do not reflect the deduction of any advisory fee and are higher than what a client would have received; a client's results would be reduced by advisory fees and any custodial or trading costs. For a complete description of advisory fees, see the Jantz Wealth Advisors fee schedule.

Worst calendar year figures are calendar-year total returns. Declines within a year, measured from peak to trough, were larger than the calendar-year figures shown. Trailing returns, yields and 2026 year-to-date figures are as of ; periods longer than one year are annualized. Five-year and ten-year returns are not available because two of the funds began trading in 2022. "Since 1/1/2022" is the start of the backtest, not the date the portfolio was first managed for clients.

Strategy risks. JEPI and JEPQ sell options to generate income, which limits how much they gain when stocks rise and does not prevent declines. JAAA and JBBB hold collateralized loan obligations (CLOs), which carry credit and liquidity risk, and BBB-rated CLOs carry more credit risk than AAA-rated CLOs. Corporate bonds can decline when interest rates rise or credit conditions weaken. "Low-volatility" describes the design goal compared with an all-stock portfolio. It does not mean the portfolio cannot decline.

Fund data. Yields and expense figures are weighted averages of the underlying fund data as of the date shown and are subject to change. Fund yields are trailing 12-month distribution yields; for JAAA and JBBB, which paid 13 times in the window, the 12 most recent monthly distributions are used.
